The answers:

  • BST Invariant Verification: The invariant dictates that for any given node, all elements located in its left sub-tree must be strictly smaller, and all elements in its right sub-tree must be strictly greater. To ensure structural integrity, an automated recursive validation function check_invariant() was executed after every single insertion operation, successfully asserting that no violations occurred across all four trees.
  • Special Property of In-order Traversal: An in-order traversal (Left → Root → Right) executed on a valid Binary Search Tree inherently outputs the contained elements in a strictly monotonically increasing (sorted) order. This occurs as a direct mathematical consequence of the BST invariant: the algorithm exhausts all smaller values by recursively drilling down the left branches, outputs the current median/root value, and then processes the strictly greater values residing in the right branches.
  • Comparison of the Large Tree: The tree generated from 77031 acts as a stark demonstration of unbalanced BST degradation. While small trees maintained reasonable heights ($h \le 9$), the large sequence produced a highly skewed structure with a height of $27$ and a node count of $351$. The depth distribution reveals a heavily bloated mid-section (maximum width of $35$ at depth 7), highlighting that without rebalancing mechanisms (like AVL rotations), structural operations trend toward worst-case linear time.
